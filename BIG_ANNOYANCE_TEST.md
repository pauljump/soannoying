# The Big Annoyance Test

So Annoying is looking for more than things somebody disliked once. The useful unit is a burden that keeps showing up in real life and still feels surprisingly unsolved.

An annoyance is a strong candidate when most of these are true:

1. **It repeats.** The person hits it often, or it returns for many people.
2. **It has a real cost.** It wastes time, money, energy, sleep, attention, access, or control.
3. **It is bigger than one bad interface.** The same burden appears across products, places, or situations.
4. **The workaround is bad.** People cannot easily avoid it, remember it, pay their way out of it, or do it manually.
5. **The complaint is concrete.** We can say who is trying to do what, what gets in the way, and what would count as better.
6. **It may have an AI opening.** The work involves context, judgment, coordination, action, or checking. This is a separate question from whether the annoyance is big.

Mosquitoes are a good example. They are recurring, widespread, physical, costly to comfort and sleep, and not solved by a single better screen. A mosquito solution may not be an AI product, but the annoyance itself is clearly big.

## What does not pass by itself

- A funny or surprising one-off
- A preference about taste or wording
- A bug tied to one obscure device with no wider pattern
- A complaint whose only evidence is strong language
- A problem that is easy to avoid with a normal workaround

This is a screening test, not a numerical ranking. Reviewers should record which parts are supported, which are guesses, and what evidence is still missing.

## Publication rule

The public page shows the records that pass this test and have a source trail that can be checked. This is a publishing decision, not a new lifecycle state. A published annoyance is not automatically a reviewed problem, an AI solution, or a validated result.
